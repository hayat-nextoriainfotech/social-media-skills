# Common tells of machine-written social copy

Fix these unless the owner's own voice uses them on purpose.

## Punctuation

- **Long dashes used as all-purpose glue.** Replace with a comma, a colon, a full stop, or two sentences.
- **Colon reveals everywhere** ("The secret: consistency."). One is fine; a pattern is a tell.
- **Exclamation marks on every line.**
- **Emoji as bullet points on every line**, unless that is the owner's style.

## Words and phrases

- Filler openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "Picture this".
- Inflated verbs and nouns: delve, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, leverage, seamless, game-changer, journey, landscape, realm, tapestry, testament.
- Hedge stacks: "it's important to note that", "it's worth mentioning".
- Generic calls to action: "Discover more today!", "Don't miss out!".
- Customer-service stiffness in replies: "Thank you for reaching out", "We appreciate your feedback", "We apologize for any inconvenience".

## Structure

- **Fragment stacks**: "Fast. Simple. Powerful." Three one-word sentences in a row.
- **Rule of three** on every list, even when there are two real points or four.
- **"It's not X, it's Y"** contrasts used as a hook every time.
- **Fake candor**: "I'll be honest", "Let me be real with you", "Here's what nobody tells you".
- **Rhetorical question, then the answer** ("The result? Growth.").
- **Summary line at the end** that repeats the post ("In short, ...").
- **Every reply the same length and shape.** Real people vary.

## Hashtags

- Hashtag walls at the end. Use a few relevant ones, or none in replies.

## Arabic

- Overly formal MSA for a casual message, or literal translations of English idioms. Match the sender's register (see `reply-in-my-voice` Arabic notes).
