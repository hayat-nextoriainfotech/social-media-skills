# Replying in Arabic

- **Follow the sender's language and register.** Gulf, Levantine, Egyptian or other dialect in, the same dialect out, as far as you can do it naturally. Modern Standard Arabic in, MSA out. If unsure, use simple, friendly MSA.
- **Mixed messages** (Arabic with English words, or Arabizi such as "shukran"): mirror the mix. Don't "correct" a casual message into formal Arabic.
- **Greetings matter.** Answer "السلام عليكم" with "وعليكم السلام" before the rest. Religious phrases (ماشاء الله، إن شاء الله، الحمد لله) are fine when the owner uses them; don't add them if the owner's voice doesn't.
- **Gender.** Arabic marks gender in verbs and pronouns. If the sender's gender is unclear, use phrasing that avoids it or a neutral plural form, and never guess from a name.
- **Numbers and facts.** Same rule as English: prices, times and dates come from the owner. Keep digits in the style the owner uses (Western 0-9 or Arabic-Indic).
- **Right-to-left text.** Keep links, @handles and numbers on their own or at the end of a sentence so they don't flip around in the reply.
- **Length.** Arabic replies often run longer than English for the same meaning. Keep the owner's usual length anyway.
